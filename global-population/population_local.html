About the data

Source. World Bank World Development Indicators: SP.POP.TOTL and population by 5-year age band and sex (SP.POP.0004.FE … SP.POP.80UP.MA), year 2025. The age series were last updated 1 July 2026; the World Bank builds them from the UN World Population Prospects.

Income level. World Bank FY2027 classification by GNI per capita (Atlas method). Every economy sits in exactly one of the four groups.

Regions. World Bank regions. The World Bank now puts Afghanistan and Pakistan with the Middle East and North Africa, so South Asia here is India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan and the Maldives.

Taiwan. The World Bank counts Taiwan in its World total but does not publish it as an economy. Its figures here are the World total minus the other 217 economies, age band by age band (23.3 million, with an age profile that matches Taiwan’s own statistics).

AI adoption. Microsoft AI Economy Institute, AI Diffusion Report, Q2 2026 update (country data published on Microsoft’s ai-diffusion-report GitHub). “AI user share” is the share of people aged 15–64 who used a generative AI product in the period, estimated from aggregated, anonymised Microsoft telemetry and adjusted for operating-system and device market share, internet access and population. It is the only measure published on one method for most of the world. Microsoft reports 18.8% worldwide for June 2026. The 72 economies it does not measure (mostly small; the largest are Yemen, North Korea and Hong Kong) are left out, not estimated. Together the measured economies hold 98.6% of the world’s 15–64s.

AI users = AI user share × World Bank 2025 population aged 15–64, so children and people over 64 are not counted. Added up, the countries give 0.99 billion users, within 2% of Microsoft’s own global figure applied to the same population (1.01 billion).

National official sources. Where a country publishes its own statistic, the page uses it: Eurostat for 35 European countries (share of 16–74s who used generative AI tools in the last 3 months, 2025), CNNIC for China, Ofcom for the UK, the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis for the US, CETIC.br for Brazil, the communications ministries of Japan and Korea, TWNIC for Taiwan and ANRT for Morocco, plus public-university surveys where no official figure exists (Australia, Canada, Singapore). Each figure is turned into users on the population that source covers. Shares of internet users are multiplied by the internet use rate. Gallup World Poll figures for African countries are listed for reference but not counted, because that study was run with Microsoft. India, Pakistan and New Zealand have no usable official figure. The national figures differ in age range, time window (last 3 months, past year, ever) and base, so read the national-versus-Microsoft gap as a range rather than a correction.

China. Microsoft says its telemetry undercounts China. CNNIC’s 57th Statistical Report (survey, December 2025) counts 602 million generative-AI users, 42.8% of the whole population. CNNIC’s later Generative AI Application Development Report 2026 estimates more than 700 million for June 2026, but that figure is a model estimate from network data, not a survey, so the page uses the survey figure.

Derived measures. Median age is interpolated within the 5-year band that holds the middle person, and is usually within a year of the UN figure. Dependency ratio = people aged 0–14 and 65+ per 100 people aged 15–64. Growth compares 2025 with 2024.
