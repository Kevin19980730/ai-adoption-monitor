Three different questions

Firms (Census BTOS): the share of employer businesses that used any AI in a business function in the last two weeks. Each firm counts once, so the many small firms dominate.

Workers (Real-Time Population Survey): the share of employed adults who use generative AI for their job.

Consumers: company-stated weekly or monthly users of ChatGPT, Gemini and Meta AI.

! BTOS changed its AI question

Until Oct 2025 the question asked about AI “in producing goods or services”. Since Dec 2025 it asks about AI “in any of its business functions”. That jump is the new wording, not real adoption. Charts show the old series dashed and never join the two. There is no data for 6 Oct–16 Nov 2025 (government shutdown).

Hyperscaler capex is late cycle, measured at a macro level

Sector capex as % of US GDP through each cycle’s speculative build phase. BofA Global Research Exhibit 2, as reprinted in BofA Institute, Can the AI buildout keep building? (27 Aug 2026). Values are read exactly from the PDF’s vector chart, not estimated from pixels. Railroads (annual, 1899–1920) sit on a compressed axis left of the break, as in the original. Sources: BofA Global Research, BEA, NBER.

The same cycles aligned on their start

Years since each series’ first point. Hyperscalers start with the four quarters to Q1 2022, so four years in they are already above telecoms’ and shale’s peaks.

Checked against the first-hand sources behind the footnote

Each panel rebuilt from primary data and compared point by point with the PDF’s vector values. Railroads: the NBER tables the footnote names. O&G and telecoms (footnote: “BEA”): BEA Fixed Assets investment by industry, all asset types, over BEA GDP. Hyperscalers: SEC filings of the five companies over BEA GDP.

How BofA dates the points: every post-1970 point is drawn three quarters early. The hyperscaler point drawn at mid-2021 is capex for the four quarters to Q1 2022, and the last point (drawn mid-2025) is the four quarters to Q1 2026. Annual year Y is drawn from mid-year Y−1 to Q1 of Y for the same reason. The dashed line below continues the first-hand hyperscaler series to the latest quarter all five have filed.

Hyperscaler capex by company, each calendar quarter

Purchases of property & equipment from each company’s 10-Q / 10-K (SEC XBRL), $bn per calendar quarter. Quarters come from year-to-date cash flows (Q4 = full year minus nine months). Oracle’s fiscal quarters end Feb / May / Aug / Nov, so it is calendarised: each calendar quarter = ⅔ × the Oracle quarter ending in its second month + ⅓ × the next one. Refreshed by the weekly job as new filings land.

Cycle by cycle

How the chart is drawn: the post-1970 panels sit on a quarterly grid. Annual series hold each year’s value from mid-year Y−1 to Q1 of Y, so peaks land in the boom years: O&G 1981, telecoms 2000, shale 2014. Hyperscaler lines change every quarter. Each is trailing-four-quarter capex of MSFT + GOOGL + AMZN + META + ORCL over GDP (see the first-hand check above; scripts in bofa-capex-cycles/firsthand/). US 10-year yield: real yield and term premium

Percent, daily. Real yield = 10-year TIPS yield (FRED DFII10, from 2003): the inflation-adjusted discount rate for long-duration assets such as AI equities. Term premium = the extra yield investors demand to hold a 10-year bond rather than roll short bills, from the Fed Board’s Kim–Wright model (FRED THREEFYTP10, from 1990; published about a week late). Nominal 10-year yield (DGS10) and breakeven inflation (nominal − real) for context. Shaded = NBER recessions.

US high-yield option-adjusted spreads, BB vs CCC & lower

ICE BofA index OAS over Treasuries, daily close, percent. FRED BAMLH0A1HYBB and BAMLH0A3HYC. FRED only serves the last 3 years (ICE licence), so 1996–2023 comes from archived copies of FRED’s full-history files; the two match exactly where they overlap. Shaded = NBER recessions.

Quality decompression: CCC minus BB

Percentage points. A widening gap while BB stays tight means stress is building in the weakest credits first. Dashed line = median since 1996.

CCC ÷ BB ratio

Times. Removes the common market level, so it isolates how much extra the weakest borrowers pay. Dashed line = median since 1996.

Latest readings and history

Changes in basis points (bp). Real yield history starts 2003, term premium 1990, nominal 1962, spreads 1996. Percentile = share of trading days at or below today’s level; low = tight / complacent, high = stressed.

Status

This is the published copy. It is refreshed automatically from the source PC after each data job (weekly on Fridays, daily for GPU prices and credit spreads).

Runs weekly_update.py, the same job as the Windows task AI Adoption Monitor Weekly (Fridays 07:00). It does three things: refreshes BTOS and the GenAI tracker (BTOS publishes every other Thursday, the tracker quarterly), rebuilds the Codex / Claude Code npm series, scans the news for new assistant user counts to review on the AI assistants tab, refreshes the OpenRouter weekly token series, adds the latest Silicon Data GPU prices (which also update daily at 08:05 on their own task), scans the news for new OpenAI / Anthropic ARR figures to review on the Lab ARR tab, refreshes the Ramp AI Index token data, and adds the latest ICE BofA high-yield spreads from FRED (which also update daily at 08:10 on their own task). Enterprise-seat estimates are still edited by hand in codex-user-trend/enterprise_seats_oct2026.csv.
